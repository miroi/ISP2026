Section 4 — Physical interpretation

The Hg₄ cluster fully dissociates on Au(111)
This is now confirmed at two slab sizes (5×5 and 9×9) with identical final structures:

A tetrahedral Hg₄ cluster on Au(111) loses its 3D structure and forms a planar 3+1 arrangement of 4 separated Hg adatoms. The three outer Hg atoms form an equilateral triangle (6.39 Å side), and the central Hg sits at the triangle's center (3.69 Å from each outer atom).

The driving force is the Hg–Au bond (per-atom E_ads ≈ −1.0 eV), which exceeds the weak Hg–Hg cohesion (~0.21 eV total for Hg₄, i.e., ~0.05 eV/atom).

Per-atom adsorption energy
System	E_ads	E_ads/Hg
Hg₁ (single adatom on fcc)	−1.17 eV	−1.17 eV
Hg₄ (4 separated adatoms)	−4.00 eV	−1.00 eV

The per-atom binding energy of the dissociated cluster (−1.00 eV) is slightly less than the single-atom value (−1.17 eV). This makes sense: in the dissociated state, the central Hg may not sit at an optimal fcc hollow (it's pinned by the 3 outer atoms), and the outer Hg atoms may be at sub-optimal sites.
